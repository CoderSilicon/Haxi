# Haxi

Find out what your symptoms could mean.

Haxi helps you understand your symptoms by checking them against trusted health information. It gives you a clear answer about what might be going on and what you can do next.

---

## What it does

**1. Safety first**
If your symptoms match emergency signs — like chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs, heavy bleeding, a seizure, or a severe allergic reaction — a red banner appears immediately. Safety does not depend on the AI being right.

**2. Check your guess**
If you think it might be a migraine, Haxi says plainly whether that fits, partly fits, or does not fit, and explains why.

**3. Answer a few questions**
Two or three short follow-up questions are generated from what you describe. Answering them and tapping through re-runs the evaluation with better information, which usually moves the answer in a useful way.

**4. Real information, not invented**
Each possible condition is looked up in the MedlinePlus Web service, a free service of the U.S. National Library of Medicine. The app links to those pages instead of writing its own medical advice. English and Spanish are both available.

**5. Browse without asking anything**
The Learn page lists conditions by body part (lungs, skin, tummy, mind, and so on) with a short plain description of each.

**6. Keep a journal**
Every result is saved, and you can add a daily check-in. A small chart shows whether you are getting better or worse.

---

## The cache

- MedlinePlus asks every client to stay under 85 requests per minute and to cache responses for 12 to 24 hours.
- Your journal is kept in your browser's local storage. It never leaves your device and is not sent to the server.

---

## Important reminder

Haxi is a learning tool, not a doctor. It cannot diagnose or treat you, and it can be wrong. Health information comes from MedlinePlus.gov, which does not endorse Haxi. If something feels dangerous, call your local emergency number straight away. For anything else, talk to a real healthcare professional.
